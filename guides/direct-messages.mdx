Direct messages in EvilChat go further than ordinary end-to-end encryption. In most E2EE chat apps, even if the relay cannot read your message content, it can still see metadata — who sent a message to whom, and which conversation it belongs to. EvilChat eliminates both leaks: **sealed-sender encryption** hands the relay a ciphertext whose outer layer is signed by a disposable ephemeral key that authenticates nobody, and the envelope is addressed to a **rotating stream tag** that changes with every encryption epoch instead of a stable conversation ID. Only the recipient can unwrap the message and learn who sent it — and only the two of you can even recognize which envelopes belong to your conversation.

## Opening a DM

You can start a DM with any peer whose identity has been announced on the relay's directory stream (i.e., anyone who has launched EvilChat at least once against the same relay).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find the person">
    Use either of these two paths:
    - Click the **search icon** in the chat header and type the person's display name or DID.
    - Open the **Members panel** in any channel, find the person, and click the **DM** button next to their name.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The DM channel opens">
    EvilChat derives a deterministic DM channel ID from the two participants' DIDs and creates a two-member MLS group for it: your client adds the peer using the KeyPackage they published on the directory stream and delivers them a sealed Welcome. If you both open the DM at the same instant, the race resolves automatically — the group created by the lower DID wins, and any early messages are re-sent through it. The deterministic ID is used only inside your clients; it never appears on the wire.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send your first message">
    Type in the compose box and press **Enter**. Your message is encrypted and dispatched as a `Sealed` envelope.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Sealed-Sender Encryption

Here is what happens under the hood when you send a DM, explained without cryptography jargon:

1. **Your message is encrypted by the DM's MLS group** — a two-member RFC 9420 group whose keys ratchet forward with every epoch, so past messages stay safe even if a device key leaks later.
2. **A fresh ephemeral keypair is generated** just for this send — it is used once and immediately discarded.
3. **The MLS ciphertext is wrapped** in an outer envelope signed by that ephemeral key and addressed to the conversation's current **epoch tag** — an opaque ID both of you derive from the group's epoch secret. From the relay's perspective, the `AuthorDid` belongs to a key it has never seen before, and the `StreamId` is a value that stops being used at the next epoch.
4. **The recipient resolves the tag** back to your conversation, decrypts through the MLS group, and finds your real DID and signature inside the ciphertext — authenticated, but only visible to them.

The relay learns neither the **source** (your DID) nor the **conversation** (the tag is unlinkable across epochs). This is the sealed-sender guarantee, extended to the stream address itself.

<Info>
DMs get **forward secrecy** from MLS epoch ratcheting and **post-compromise security** from a periodic self-update: every 7 days one side (deterministically, the lower DID) rotates its keys, which also rotates the stream tag. EvilChat chose two-member MLS over Signal's Double Ratchet to keep a single audited crypto stack — see the [encryption model](/security/encryption-model) for the full reasoning.
</Info>

## The DMs Sidebar Tab

All your DM conversations live in the **DMS** tab in the left sidebar. Click it to see your full list of direct conversations, sorted by most recent activity. Unread counts appear as badges on each entry.

From the DMS tab you can:
- Click any conversation to jump directly to it.
- See the last message preview (your client decrypts it locally; the preview never leaves your device).

## DM vs Channel

| | Direct Message | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| **Participants** | Two people | Any number of members |
| **Sender privacy** | Always sealed — relay sees neither sender nor conversation | Standard: sender device DID visible on the wire. Sealed channels: same guarantees as DMs |
| **Encryption** | Two-member MLS group under sealed sender | MLS group (`Kind = Mls`, or sealed if the channel opts in) |
| **Wire address** | Rotating epoch tag | Stable channel GUID (standard) or rotating epoch tag (sealed) |
| **Forward secrecy** | MLS epochs + periodic self-update | MLS epochs + membership churn (owners self-update sealed channels) |
| **Moderation** | N/A | Owner + moderators can ban (MLS Remove — cryptographic ejection) |
