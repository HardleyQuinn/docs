Direct messages in EvilChat go further than ordinary end-to-end encryption. In most E2EE chat apps, even if the relay cannot read your message content, it can still see metadata — specifically, who sent a message to whom. EvilChat eliminates that leak with **sealed-sender encryption**: the relay receives a ciphertext whose outer layer is signed by a disposable ephemeral key that authenticates nobody. Only the recipient can unwrap the inner layer and learn who actually sent it.

## Opening a DM

You can start a DM with any peer whose identity has been announced on the relay's directory stream (i.e., anyone who has launched EvilChat at least once against the same relay).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find the person">
    Use either of these two paths:
    - Click the **search icon** in the chat header and type the person's display name or DID.
    - Open the **Members panel** in any channel, find the person, and click the **DM** button next to their name.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The DM channel opens">
    EvilChat derives a deterministic DM channel ID from the two participants' DIDs. Because the ID is deterministic, no negotiation handshake is needed — both sides independently compute the same channel ID and begin writing to the same stream.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send your first message">
    Type in the compose box and press **Enter**. Your message is encrypted and dispatched as a `Sealed` envelope.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Sealed-Sender Encryption

Here is what happens under the hood when you send a DM, explained without cryptography jargon:

1. **Your message is encrypted** with the DM channel's shared RoomKey (AES-256-GCM), the same way a channel message would be.
2. **A fresh ephemeral keypair is generated** just for this send — it is used once and immediately discarded.
3. **The encrypted payload is re-wrapped** in an outer envelope signed by that ephemeral key. From the relay's perspective, the outer `AuthorDid` field belongs to a key it has never seen before and will never see again. The relay cannot link the envelope to you.
4. **The recipient unwraps the outer layer** and finds your real DID, your real signature, and your message inside the ciphertext — authenticated, but only visible to them.

The relay knows the **destination** (the recipient's inbox stream ID) but not the **source** (your DID). This is the sealed-sender guarantee.

<Info>
The current sealed-sender DM scheme encrypts over a static RoomKey shared for the lifetime of the DM conversation. A **Double Ratchet** session — which would give every individual message its own independent key and full forward secrecy — is on the roadmap. Until then, compromising the DM RoomKey would expose the full conversation history for that channel.
</Info>

## The DMs Sidebar Tab

All your DM conversations live in the **DMS** tab in the left sidebar. Click it to see your full list of direct conversations, sorted by most recent activity. Unread counts appear as badges on each entry.

From the DMS tab you can:
- Click any conversation to jump directly to it.
- See the last message preview (your client decrypts it locally; the preview never leaves your device).

## DM vs Channel

| | Direct Message | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| **Participants** | Two people | Any number of members |
| **Sender privacy** | Relay cannot see sender (sealed-sender) | Relay cannot see content; sender DID is visible in envelope metadata |
| **Encryption** | Sealed-sender over RoomKey | RoomKey (AES-256-GCM) or MLS group |
| **Channel ID** | Deterministic from two DIDs | Randomly generated GUID |
| **Forward secrecy** | Roadmap (Double Ratchet) | MLS channels only |
| **Moderation** | N/A | Owner + moderators can ban |
