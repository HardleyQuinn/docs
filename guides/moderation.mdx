EvilChat moderation is built on the same append-only event model as messaging — bans and moderator grants are cryptographic facts appended to the relay stream, not state the relay stores or controls. The relay knows nothing about them. Every moderation action is a signed event; every authority check is computed locally by the client at the moment you query the member list or load a channel. This means moderation works offline, converges across all clients without coordination, and produces a complete, tamper-evident audit trail.

## Who Can Moderate

Moderation is **server-wide**. Channels have no owners — the only authority figures in a community are the server owner and the moderators they appoint:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Server Owner" icon="crown">
    The DID that created the community's `server-control` channel — effectively the relay host. The server owner can ban and unban members (per channel or community-wide), grant and revoke moderator status, create channels, and edit channel settings.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Moderator" icon="shield">
    A member the server owner has explicitly promoted. Moderators hold authority in **every** channel: they can ban and unban members, delete messages, create channels, and edit channel settings. They **cannot** grant moderator status to other members — there are no transitive grants.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Regular members have no moderation authority, and creating a channel confers none.

## Banning a Member

Banning a member appends a `MemberBannedEvent` to the channel stream. The banned member's future messages are rejected by other clients' projections; they cannot participate in the channel until unbanned.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Members panel">
    Click the **person-list icon** in the channel's top bar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select the member">
    Click the member's name or avatar to expand their profile card.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ban">
    Click **Ban from channel** (or **Server ban** to apply the ban community-wide — see [Server-Wide Bans](#server-wide-bans) below). Confirm when prompted.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**To lift a ban**, follow the same steps and click **Unban**. This appends a new `MemberBannedEvent` with `Lifted: true`. Because authority is evaluated at query time using the highest `(Timestamp, EventId)` among valid ban events for the target, the unban wins deterministically regardless of the order events arrive on other clients.

## Bans Are Cryptographic, Not Just Social

A ban does two things. First, honest clients suppress the banned member's messages — that is the convergent social agreement described above. Second, and stronger: the channel creator's client (the one that runs the channel's membership loop) issues an **MLS Remove commit**, ratcheting the group to a new epoch whose keys the banned member cannot derive. From that epoch on, the banned member cannot *decrypt* channel traffic at all, even though they can still append ciphertext to the public relay (proof-of-work and rate limits bound the flood; honest clients ignore it).

In a [sealed channel](/security/sealed-mode), the Remove commit itself rides a sealed envelope — the relay never observes that a membership operation happened, let alone who was ejected. If an unban follows, the member is re-added through their published KeyPackage with a fresh Welcome, exactly like a first join; messages sent while they were banned remain undecryptable to them permanently.

<Note>
Honest limits, stated plainly: a tombstoned or banned member's *pre-ban* messages and keys are not clawed back — anything they could decrypt before the ban, they keep. The Remove commit protects everything after it.
</Note>

## Granting Moderator Status

Only the server owner can grant or revoke moderator status, and the grant applies across the entire community — there is no per-channel moderator role.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a member's card">
    Click the member anywhere they appear — the Members panel, people search, or a message avatar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Grant moderator">
    Click **Make Mod**. This appends a `ModeratorGrantedEvent` to the `server-control` stream, signed by you (the server owner).
  </Step>
</Steps>

To **revoke** moderator status, follow the same steps — the button shows **Revoke Mod** for existing moderators. This appends a `ModeratorGrantedEvent` with `Revoked: true`. Only server-owner–authored grant/revoke events on the `server-control` stream count; moderators cannot promote others, and grant events on ordinary channel streams are ignored by every honest client.

## How Authority is Resolved

No server validates moderation events. Instead, each client independently evaluates authority at **query time** — when it renders the member list, processes a message, or checks whether a ban is in effect.

The resolution algorithm:

1. For each `(channel, target)` pair, collect all `MemberBannedEvent` records.
2. Filter to only those whose `AuthorDid` holds server-wide authority: the server owner (the creator of the `server-control` channel) or a member holding the winning, unrevoked server-owner–authored grant on that stream.
3. The surviving event with the highest `(Timestamp, EventId)` is the current ban state.

This means:
- **Out-of-order delivery is safe.** If a moderator-grant event arrives after a ban event issued by that same moderator, the projection re-evaluates and the ban is correctly validated retroactively.
- **There is no global lock.** Multiple clients compute the same result from the same event stream — convergence is guaranteed by the deterministic sort key.

<Note>
The relay has no knowledge of bans whatsoever. Bans are purely client-side projections derived from the event stream. A banned member can still technically post signed envelopes to the relay — but every client that has processed the ban event will silently discard those envelopes when projecting the channel view.
</Note>

## Server-Wide Bans

For serious situations — spam, abuse, or bad actors who should be removed from the entire community — you can issue a **server ban** instead of a per-channel ban. Server bans are `MemberBannedEvent` records posted to the special **`server-control`** channel stream rather than an individual channel's stream.

Every client projects the `server-control` stream (it is server-public — everyone must receive ban events to apply them) and applies those bans across every channel simultaneously. The channel itself is hidden from navigation; only its events matter.

The `server-control` stream is also where the community's authority is anchored: its creator **is** the server owner, and moderator grants only count when they ride this stream.

<Warning>
Server bans are as final as channel bans — they can be lifted with a `Lifted: true` event, but until lifted, the banned DID cannot participate in any channel in the community. Use server bans deliberately.
</Warning>
