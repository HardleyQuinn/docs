Channels are named, persistent, end-to-end encrypted group spaces where any number of members can exchange messages, reactions, and shared media. Every message posted to a channel is encrypted before it leaves your device — the relay stores and forwards opaque ciphertext and never reads a word of your conversation. Creating a channel is instant, and the encryption setup happens automatically in the background.

## Creating a Channel

<Steps>
  <Step title="Click the + button">
    In the left sidebar, click the **+** button next to the Channels heading. The new-channel dialog opens.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter a name">
    Type a name for your channel. Channel names are encrypted in transit — the name is only visible to members of the channel's MLS group.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose sealed mode (optional)">
    Check **sealed** to hide the channel from the relay entirely: every envelope it produces — messages, the creation event, even membership operations — is signed by a disposable key and addressed to a rotating stream tag. The relay cannot tell the channel exists. Leave it unchecked for a standard channel, whose ciphertext rides a stable stream ID. See [Sealed mode](/security/sealed-mode) for the trade-offs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Click **Create**. EvilChat bootstraps a fresh MLS group with you as its first member. The channel appears in your sidebar and you are its owner.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Members join automatically">
    v1 channels are server-public: your client automatically adds every announced member of the community to the group through their published KeyPackage, delivering each a sealed MLS Welcome. Members who announce later are added as they appear. Invite-only channels are a roadmap item.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How Encryption Works

Every channel is an **MLS group** (RFC 9420), run by the `evil-mls` Rust core. MLS provides:

- **Epoch-based group state** — every add, remove, or update triggers a key ratchet (Commit), so new keys are not derived from old ones.
- **Cryptographic membership** — banning a member triggers a Commit that ejects them from the group; they cannot decrypt future messages even if they retain every key they ever held.
- **Forward secrecy** — each epoch uses independently derived keying material, and old message secrets are deleted.

New members receive an MLS **Welcome** message sealed to their inbox. The Welcome carries enough state to join the group at the current epoch, plus the channel's name and policy flags so your client can show the channel immediately.

<Note>
Channels created by early versions of EvilChat used a static shared **RoomKey** instead of MLS. Those channels were migrated automatically by their owners' clients; the old ciphertext still decrypts, but nothing encrypts that way anymore. See the [encryption model](/security/encryption-model#legacy-roomkey-ciphertext).
</Note>

## Joining a Channel

You cannot browse or request to join channels. In v1, channel owners' clients add every announced community member automatically; when that happens for you, the relay delivers a **KeySeal** envelope to your personal inbox stream containing an `MlsWelcomeEvent` sealed to your X25519 seal key.

Your client decrypts the sealed envelope, joins the MLS group at the current epoch, and publishes a fresh KeyPackage (the one just consumed is spent). From that point, new channel traffic decrypts in real time. Messages from epochs before you joined are not decryptable — that is forward secrecy working as intended.

<Note>
**"Park, don't drop."** If a channel message arrives at your client before its Welcome does — or, in a sealed channel, arrives under a stream tag you cannot resolve yet — the ciphertext is stored locally and replayed automatically the moment the key material lands. You will never lose a message to a race condition.
</Note>

## Channel Members

Open the **Members panel** by clicking the person-list icon in the top bar of any channel. The panel shows:

- A count of members who hold the channel key.
- Which members are currently online (green dot) vs. offline.
- Per-member actions: **DM**, **Grant Moderator**, **Ban** (if you have authority).
