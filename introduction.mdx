EvilChat is a decentralized, end-to-end encrypted chat application built on the **EVIL** (Event Verified Immutable Ledger) protocol. Traditional messaging platforms centralize identity — you are a username in a database, authenticated by a password the server holds. EvilChat discards that model entirely. Your identity is a self-sovereign Ed25519 keypair that your device generates and never shares. Your messages are AES-256-GCM ciphertext that only the intended recipients can decrypt. The servers in the middle — the relays — are deliberately blind: they receive opaque signed envelopes, assign a monotonic offset, and serve them back in chronological order. They run no business logic, verify no signatures, and read no content. Every feature of EvilChat — message history, reactions, edits, deletions, bans, moderator grants — is an event appended to an immutable log and projected into a local read model on your device.

## What Makes EvilChat Different

Most chat apps trade privacy for convenience: your messages live in plaintext on a company's servers, your account is tied to a phone number or email, and the provider can read, filter, or delete anything. EvilChat makes different trade-offs:

- **No accounts.** Identity is a keypair. There is no registration flow, no password to reset, and no central authority that can lock you out.
- **Relay-blind encryption.** Relays store ciphertext and nothing else. Even if a relay is compromised or subpoenaed, there is no plaintext to hand over.
- **Event-sourced state.** All state — channel membership, message history, bans, reactions — is derived from an append-only log of signed, encrypted events. Clients project state locally; the relay stores facts.
- **Sealed-sender DMs.** Direct messages use a per-message ephemeral outer key so the relay cannot determine who sent a DM, only that one was sent.

## Core Concepts

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Identity (DID + Keypair)" icon="fingerprint">
    Your identity is an Ed25519 keypair generated on your device. Your DID is `did:evil:<base64url(pubkey)>`. You also hold an X25519 key that others use to seal MLS Welcomes to you. A root key signs device subkeys — your root key signs only device authority delegations.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Relay (Dumb Pipe)" icon="network-wired">
    A relay accepts signed envelopes via `POST /events`, assigns a monotonic arrival offset, and serves them via `GET /events?since=&limit=`. It checks size, PoW difficulty, and per-DID rate limits — nothing more. It never verifies signatures or inspects payloads.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Channels (MLS group encryption)" icon="lock">
    Every channel is an RFC 9420 MLS group with epoch-based key ratcheting; banning a member cryptographically ejects them. Channels can opt into sealed mode, hiding even their existence from the relay. Moderation — bans, moderator grants — is enforced client-side at query time.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Vault (Permanent Archive)" icon="database">
    The optional `evilvault` daemon is a self-hosted CLI + HTTP server that receives Storage-Key-encrypted records from your client and stores them in a local archive on your machine. It never holds decryption keys. You control the storage, the token, and the retention policy.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Features

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="End-to-End Encrypted Channels" icon="shield-halved">
    Every channel message is encrypted before it leaves your device. New members receive an MLS Welcome via a sealed `KeySeal` envelope addressed to their inbox stream.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Sealed-Sender Direct Messages" icon="envelope">
    DMs use a per-message ephemeral outer keypair. The relay sees only that a `Sealed` envelope was posted — it cannot identify the sender.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Reactions, Edits & Deletes" icon="pen-to-square">
    Reactions, message edits, and deletions are appended facts, not mutations. Edits converge on the highest `(Timestamp, EventId)` pair; a deletion tombstone prevents a late `MessageSent` from resurrecting a deleted message.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Moderation" icon="gavel">
    Channel owners can ban/unban members and grant moderator status. Authority is resolved client-side at query time — out-of-order grant and ban events still converge correctly.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Command Palette" icon="magnifying-glass">
    Press **⌘K** (or **Ctrl+K**) anywhere in the app to open the command palette. Create channels, switch conversations, or trigger actions without touching the mouse.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Dark & Light Theme" icon="circle-half-stroke">
    EvilChat supports both dark and light modes. Your preference is stored locally and applied immediately — no account setting to sync.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## The EVIL Protocol

<Note>
  **EVIL** stands for **Event Verified Immutable Ledger**. The protocol defines the wire format (`SignedEnvelope`), the five envelope kinds, the proof-of-work stamp, the canonical signature preimage, and the set of architecture rules every compliant client and relay must follow. The core rules are: relays run zero business logic; signatures are verified before decryption; out-of-order ciphertext is parked and replayed when keys arrive; cursors advance by relay offset, never by author-asserted timestamps; and the Vault archives Storage-Key-encrypted plaintext, never transient network ciphertext. These rules together guarantee that the event log is an authoritative, tamper-evident source of truth that any client can independently project.
</Note>

The full EVIL stack in EvilChat includes Ed25519 device-subkey identity, MLS group encryption everywhere (bans cryptographically eject members at the protocol layer), sealed-sender DMs and sealed channels addressed by rotating epoch stream tags, a two-tier proof-of-work relay gate, per-author event chaining with gap detection, and the self-hostable `evilvault` archive daemon with hydration support.
